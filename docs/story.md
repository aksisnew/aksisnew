<h1 align="center">My Journey in Tech</h1>

<p align="center">
  <i>"Tech was never about the grind or the paycheck. It was the spark that taught me how to live."</i>
</p>

<hr/>

<p>
  It all started when I was twelve, sitting in front of my old HP laptop with wide eyes and endless curiosity. I wasn't trying to build a career or calculate future salaries—I was just mesmerized by the sheer magic of creation. Scrolling through tech videos, watching lines of code transform into working tools, I fell in love with Python. It wasn't work; it was pure, unadulterated wonder.
</p>

<p>
  To me, tech can never be just about money. If you strip away the passion, you lose the soul of it. But make no mistake: I am not a one-dimensional nerd whose entire existence revolves around a screen. Life is far too vast for that. I’ve always held onto the firm belief that having passions, interests, and creative outlets outside of tech and studying is what keeps us human, grounded, and fulfilled.
</p>

<p>
  By thirteen, my curiosity pushed me toward the web. I started tinkering with minimal HTML, CSS, and JavaScript—stitching together simple layouts and watching elements come alive on a browser. That quiet, steady exploration carried me through my early teens, slowly building my foundation at 14, 15, and 16.
</p>

<p>
  When I turned sixteen, I felt a familiar pull back to my roots and re-immersed myself in Python. By 17 and 18, everything clicked into place. I stepped into real full-stack web development—building robust backends, structuring workflows, and shaping complete digital environments.
</p>

<p>
  Looking back, tech gave me something far greater than code or terminal commands. It completely transformed my perspective on life. It taught me that life isn't just a rat race of endless studying or chasing milestones. It’s about connecting with people, embracing creative freedom, enjoying the journey, and making every single day count.
</p>
